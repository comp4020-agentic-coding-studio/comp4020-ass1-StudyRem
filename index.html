    Why can't this go on forever?

    Every step above didn't just add one block — the model re-read the whole transcript, every time, from scratch. That doesn't scale. Here's the same loop, running on its own against a fixed-size context window:

    • Every call re-sends the whole history — not just what's new. A 20-step session re-bills the first tool's output 19 extra times.
    • Cost and latency scale with total tokens in context — self-attention means more tokens costs more compute per generated token, not just more to read.
    • Quality degrades before the hard cutoff — "lost in the middle": detail buried deep in a huge context gets attended to worse than detail near the edges.
    • There's a hard trained limit regardless — beyond it, behaviour isn't reliable, however much you're willing to pay.
    • The fix: truncate old tool output, summarise/compact history, or move things to files instead of live context.

    That's compaction: cheaper, but not free. Whatever just got squeezed into a stub, the model can no longer actually see — only the fact that something happened there.

    This is why real agent harnesses quietly summarise, truncate, or move history to files — not as a nicety, but because the alternative is a context window that either bankrupts you or forgets the part that mattered. What looks like memory in an agent session isn't memory at all. It's a transcript, replayed in full, until someone decides what to cut.
