    Why can't this go on forever?

    Every step above didn't just add one block — the model re-read the whole transcript, every time, from scratch. That doesn't scale. Here's the same loop, running on its own against a fixed-size context window:

    • Every call re-sends the whole history — not just what's new. A 20-step session re-bills the first tool's output 19 extra times.
    • Cost and latency scale with total tokens in context — self-attention means more tokens costs more compute per generated token, not just more to read.
    • Quality degrades before the hard cutoff — "lost in the middle": detail buried deep in a huge context gets attended to worse than detail near the edges.
    • There's a hard trained limit regardless — beyond it, behaviour isn't reliable, however much you're willing to pay.
    • The fix: several, actually — more on that further down.

    So what do real agents actually do?

    None of this is exotic — every serious agent harness carries some combination of these, usually more than one at once:

    • Truncate old tool output — keep the last N lines of a huge log or diff instead of the whole thing; most of it was never going to matter again.
    • Summarise or compact history — when the window fills, collapse older turns into a shorter summary instead of a bare stub, so at least the gist survives eviction.
    • Retrieve instead of carrying everything live — keep the bulk of a codebase or document set out of context entirely, and pull in only the parts a search says are relevant to this turn (RAG).
    • Delegate to sub-agents — hand a big, self-contained chunk of work to a fresh context of its own, and bring back only its final answer, not its whole working transcript.
    • Offload to files — write intermediate results to disk and re-read them on demand, so they exist without occupying the live window the whole time.
